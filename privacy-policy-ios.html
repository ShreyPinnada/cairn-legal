Cairn

Privacy Policy — iPhone & iPad

Last updated: 25 September 2026

Cairn is a place to write down your thoughts. This policy explains what happens to them.

The short version: your thoughts stay on your iPhone. Cairn has no account, no server and no analytics. Nobody — including the developer — can read what you write.

What Cairn collects about you

Nothing, and the developer receives nothing.

There is no sign-up, no profile and no user ID. Cairn does not collect, transmit or store your thoughts, your searches, your questions, your location, your voice or your photos on any server. There is no analytics or advertising SDK, no tracking and no third-party profiling. Your writing is never used to train any model.

Where your thoughts live

On your iPhone, in Cairn's private storage, which other apps cannot read.

Everything Cairn appears to "understand" about your writing — labels, moods, themes, connections, search — is computed on the device. Voice notes are transcribed on the device; Cairn requires on-device recognition, so the audio is never sent to Apple for transcription. Text in photos is read by Apple's Vision framework, also on the device.

Apple Intelligence. On an iPhone that has Apple Intelligence turned on, Cairn uses Apple's on-device language model to name themes, fill in a mood the word list could not decide, and word short summaries. This runs on your iPhone. Cairn does not use Private Cloud Compute or any server model. Thoughts that Cairn keeps quiet about (see Sensitive writing) are never given to it.

When Cairn does use the internet

For exactly these four things. None of them send your thoughts.

1. iCloud backup — only if you turn it on. Backups are encrypted on your iPhone and written to Cairn's own folder in your iCloud Drive. The backup is your data in your Apple Account; the developer has no access to it and no copy of it. Turn backup off, or delete the backup from iCloud, at any time.

2. Downloading Cairn's on-device search model. The model that lets search understand meaning is downloaded once after install, to keep the app smaller. It is fetched from a public GitHub releases page. Nothing about you or your writing is sent. As with downloading any file, the server can see your IP address and the time of the request. Cairn sends no identifier with it.

3. Opening a map link you shared. If you share a short map link into Cairn (for example a maps.app.goo.gl link), Cairn follows that link's redirects to work out which place it points to. That request goes to the map service the link belongs to and contains only the link you shared — nothing you wrote.

4. Purchases. Buying Cairn+ is handled entirely by Apple through the App Store. Cairn never sees your payment details.

iPhone search — off by default

Cairn can make your thoughts findable from Spotlight, your iPhone's own search. This is off unless you turn it on in Search and privacy, and you can turn it off whenever you want.

While it is on, the text and date of your thoughts are placed in the iPhone's Spotlight index. That index stays on your iPhone and is not sent anywhere — but it sits outside Cairn's private storage and answers before Cairn is opened.

Because of that:

  • Turning it off empties the index. Off means the copies are removed, not only that Cairn stops adding to them.
  • It cannot be on at the same time as the app lock. Spotlight answers before Face ID is asked, so the two would cancel each other out. Turning the app lock on withdraws your thoughts from Spotlight; turning it off again restores your choice.
  • Archived thoughts are never included.
  • Only the text and the date are shared — never moods, labels, places, voice notes or images.

Permissions, and why

Each is optional, asked for only when you use the feature, and used on the device only.

PermissionWhy
MicrophoneRecording a voice note, while you hold the button
Speech recognitionTurning a voice note into text, on the device
Location (while using)Tagging a thought with where you were, only when you tap the pin
NotificationsReminders and the evening nudge; turn them off in settings
Face IDThe optional app lock. Cairn never sees your face data; iOS only tells it yes or no

Photos are chosen with the system picker, which gives Cairn only the photo you pick — Cairn has no access to your library. Denying any permission leaves the rest of Cairn working.

Your control over your data

  • Export everything, any time, as an encrypted file you keep — free, forever.
  • Delete anything, or delete the app. Deleting the app removes your thoughts from the iPhone. If you use iCloud backup, delete the backup from iCloud as well.
  • No lock-in. Everything you wrote stays readable, searchable and exportable whether or not you ever buy Cairn+.

Because Cairn has no account and no server, there is no database anywhere for the developer to delete on your behalf — and nothing to leak.

Sensitive writing

People write hard things in a private journal. Cairn is designed for that.

Cairn checks your writing against a list of crisis-related phrases entirely on your iPhone so that it can stay quiet about those thoughts — it will not bring them back unasked, quote them in summaries, give them to Apple Intelligence, or put them in Spotlight. Nothing is shown to you, nothing is recorded about it, nothing is sent anywhere, and no diagnosis of any kind is made. It never hides anything from you: your own search and export always show everything you wrote.

Children

Cairn is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information from them. Since Cairn collects no personal information from anyone, there is nothing collected from a child either.

Changes to this policy

If Cairn ever starts collecting anything, this policy will say so before that version ships, and the App Store privacy label will change in the same release.
